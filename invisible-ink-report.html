Invisible-ink survey: the onion skin letters

Every onion skin letter in the 1940–42 corpus, page by page, checked for very light writing that is not blotted over: faint pencil, faded ink, marginalia, text in other orientations or languages. Blots were not processed. 22 letters, 48 pages, zero invisible-ink candidates. Two marginal continuations are recorded as potential findings; both read as ordinary visible-ink margin-filling.

01

The brief

Run the lab’s tests on the onion skin letters, including the one with the Opole address, looking for invisible ink: not blotted over, just very light. Process each onion skin letter per the HH spreadsheet; skip no letters; do not process blots. Text may run in other directions or languages. Anything unusual gets recorded as a potential finding.

  • Corpus: all 54 letter PDFs pulled from the Drive vault. First-page triage identified 22 on thin translucent stock (show-through visible): 8, 9, 12, 24, 25, 27, 28, 29, 33, 34, 35, 37, 38, 40, 41, 42, 46, 47, 48, 52, 53, 54.
  • Blots present but not processed: 25 (black blot p1), 40 (writer’s own wet re-tracing, not a redaction), 46 (pen dots), 47 (pen-spit dots), 53 (two black blots), 54 (heavy black blots over the closing).
  • Rule for show-through: onion skin always ghosts the reverse. Only marks that do not match the reverse side count; mirrored letterforms aligned with the opposite page were dismissed.
  • Over-report rule: anything unusual that could not be dismissed was recorded as a potential finding, even at low confidence.
02

Method

A four-panel diagnostic per page, rendered at 150 DPI: original, faint-mark overlay, Gabor ridges, faint-contrast stretch. Script: blot-lab/scripts/invisible_ink.py.

  • Faint-mark overlay: local-deviation gate — pixels darker than their immediate surroundings by 3–18 lightness levels, kept only as connected stroke-like structures (area ≥ 12 px). Painted red over the original. Robust to paper shading gradients that defeat a global lightness band.
  • Gabor ridges: max response over 4 orientations (λ8), normalized. Finds thin stroke ridges even at very low contrast.
  • Faint-contrast stretch: lightness range 190–250 stretched to full scale. The most sensitive single view for faded pencil and washed-out ink.
  • Review: every strip inspected in full; suspicious margin and edge marks re-examined at 200–300 DPI crops. Per-page stats (faint-mark fraction, Gabor energy) recorded for triage.
03

Potential findings

Two marks survived dismissal and are recorded per the over-report rule. Both are ordinary visible-ink marginalia.

Why these are not invisible ink. Both sit in the main ink of their letters, in the writer’s own hand, continuing the correspondence. They were flagged only because they run vertically in the margin — exactly the “other directions” the brief asked to catch.

05

The Opole sheet (letter 40)

The sheet with the address notes, checked at 200 DPI with 2× zoom on the note regions.

  • Front, bottom: the known green pencil note — “G. Gerhardi per Adr. Norddeutscher Lloyd Bremen” plus “eine bekannte von mir bekomt von die Adresse Pakete”. No additional faint writing around it.
  • Mid-page: words gone over repeatedly in heavy wet blue ink (“Schiffskarten … Pakete an Josef schicken”) plus a dark blue blob at left. The writer’s own re-tracing/emphasis of visible text, not a redaction; left unprocessed per the no-blot rule.
  • Reverse, bottom: the known graphite note “Opole Kreis Pulawy distrikt Lublin Distr.(?)”. The faint green ghosts overlapping it are the Gerhardi note showing through from the front (mirrored). Nothing else faint present.
Verdict: the Opole sheet carries exactly the two known notes. No invisible-ink layer, no third hand, no additional addresses.
06

Open questions

  • The two marginal continuations (8, 9) are transcribed only approximately; a closer read at higher resolution could firm up the wording if it matters.
  • Letter 46’s second hand (p4–p7, signed “Lea Frohlich” ?) is ordinary co-writing, but the signature reading is uncertain.
  • Duplicate scans (35 p1/p2, 38 p2/p3) are worth flagging to Helen’s audit.
  • Physical re-photography (raking light, transmitted light) remains the only route that could beat these digital results — the survey is limited to what the existing photographs captured.
