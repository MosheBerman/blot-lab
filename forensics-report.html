Forensic sweep: what the blot-lab tools see in the letters

Every tool on the lab bench, run against four targets: the scan-17 redaction block, the letter-39 blue blot, the letter-45 Berliner scribble (the positive control), and postcard board vs onion skin. Each permutation below was run and judged; nothing is invented, and the settings are recorded so anyone can reproduce them.

Scan 17: the dotted rows in the block

In the chroma hue mask, two rough dotted horizontal rows cross the black block. 52 permutations tested whether they are under-strokes.

  • Best isolation: hue center 105, width 8. Width 4 thins the rows; width 16 densifies but adds blob noise.
  • Chroma at 105/8: Saturation view hides the rows entirely; Hue view shows them as brighter dotted bands.
  • Directional tab (Rows, k 1.0–2.5 × window 15–61, 16 combos): no combination isolated the rows. The filter responds to all horizontal handwriting texture, which overwhelms the faint signal.
  • Gabor tab (ink axis, orientation 0, λ 4/6/8/12, gain 6): not isolated at any wavelength.
  • Ridge tab (dark, sensitivity 0.01/0.02/0.05): not isolated at any sensitivity.
Tracing the dots settles it. Replicating the lab’s chroma mask exactly (a pure hue gate) and extracting dot centroids: all 141 dots sit within 3 px of the block’s edge, median distance zero. None in the block interior, none on open paper. The “two rows” are the top edge of the upper stroke and the bottom edge of the lower stroke, traced in edge pixels where the black ink feathers out. Unioning nearby hue centers (95–125) adds dots but they stay edge-locked. The same result holds on a cropped re-run (126 dots, all within 3 px).
Positive control: the same gate on legible writing. Running the identical hue-105/8 dot extraction on the letter-45 Berliner crop (blue handwriting) puts 324 dots on the ink, tracing the letterforms. The method does find writing when writing is there; on the block it finds only edge pixels.
02

Scan 17: 3D stack and Join projections

14 stack permutations (7 algorithms × Window/Advanced, 12 layers, sheet spacing 35) and 5 Join projections.

  • 3D stack: the redaction strokes never appear as solid strokes. Emboss and Raking show dense cursive script in relief across every layer (the visible writing); Bands, A2 gate, Chroma, Gabor and Direct. show mostly blank, speckled or textured sheets.
  • Join: Bands gives sparse speckles; A2 gate renders empty (no joined spans); Chroma shows short horizontal fragments plus one blotch right of center; Direct. gives sparse dots; Ridge gives the only structured view, a dense ridge network.
  • The Ridge network traces the visible writing’s contours. It is the most detailed structural view of the block area, but it does not resolve into letterforms of covered text.
03

Letter 39: the blue blot

66 permutations. The question: does anything show under or through the blue blot, and what is its topography?

  • Chroma (Mask, 21 combos): hue 95–110 captures the blue ink strongly (denser with wider width); 115–125 misses it. Densest at 110/16.
  • Gabor (luminance, gain 6): wavelength 6 gives strong metallic embossed relief at all orientations (0/45/90/135); wavelength 10 gives sparse faint dashes.
  • A2 gate (Kept, 18 combos): blue mode at saturation 200 reduces the blot to small dark fragments; handwriting stays. Nothing legible underneath.
  • Directional (window 31): Rows and Cols at k 1.5/2.5 give bold strokes on noise; no under-structure.
  • Emboss (cutoff 8/16 × angle 0/90): black relief at 0°, silver relief at 90°. The blot reads as a rough mottled, pitted zone.
  • Raking (gain 5, azimuth 0/90/180/270): bright relief strokes at all angles; 90° gives the strongest 3D texture. The blot is rough, bumpy, mottled.
  • 3D stack (Advanced, 12 layers, 7 algorithms): ink-distribution views only; no hidden text.
Verdict: negative. No combination revealed hidden structure under the blue blot. Topography: a raised, rough, granular, cratered/pitted relief zone, clearly distinct from the smoother surrounding paper in Emboss and Raking.

Letter 45: the Berliner scribble (positive control)

66 permutations. The scribble covers the word “Berliner”; the known under-text reads “er” at the end. This is the control that proves the tools can work.

  • The deblot: A2 gate, Ink=Blue, Saturation threshold 200 (at lightness 80, 100, 120) heavily removes the dark-blue scribble and exposes faint lighter-blue under-strokes in the scribble zone, including at the “er” position. Clearest at Blue/100/200 and Blue/120/200.
  • Around the capital B: the clearest deblots show white gaps where the scribble was removed, with faint original handwriting strokes visible in the gaps. The B itself was never fully covered.
  • Chroma (21 combos): same hue response as the 39 blot, 95–110 strong, 115–125 weak. No under-text beyond the A2 result.
  • Gabor (λ6): strong metallic relief, “der Berliner” legible with the scribble as a dark blob. λ10: sparse dashes.
  • Directional: Cols at k 2.5 gives the clearest vertical stroke structures; Rows views are noisy.
  • Emboss 8/0: excellent detail, “der Berliner” very legible. Raking 270°: very high contrast, very legible. These two give the most readable views of the original text.
  • 3D stack (7 algorithms): ink distribution only; no additional hidden text.
Why this matters. The Berliner result is the calibration the negatives stand on: the same A2 gate that peels the scribble here finds nothing to peel on scan 17 or letter 39. The tools are not blind; the other targets are genuinely opaque.

Postcard board vs onion skin

Five images, now built-in lab samples: scan-5 address and message sides, the full scan-17 postcard, and both pages of the letter-8 onion skin. 18 permutations per image.

  • Bands in Window mode at low positions is the most reliable isolator everywhere: paper texture suppressed, ink strokes solid white-on-black. Polarity flips around position 6 and gets noisy.
  • Gabor at 45° (luminance, λ8, gain 6) is the best orientation-independent enhancer: crisp readable text on all five.
  • Chroma contributes nothing for black handwriting on either substrate; it only pulls colored postal markings (stamps, postmarks).
  • Directional Rows amplifies noise far more than signal on all five images.
  • Substrate comparison: onion skin isolates better. Its thin, uniform, low-texture sheet renders ink as bold solid strokes with almost no paper interference. Postcard board also isolates well with Bands, but heavier texture and competing printed elements (stamps, postmarks, address rules) demand tighter band choices.
06

Scale, fibers, and reversing the grain

How big is a pixel here, can paper fibers be resolved, and can the board’s grain be undone?

  • Scale, corrected. The old 90 µm/px figure was never anchored to a reference object. Two independent rulers on the scan-17 address side agree: the card width (A6, 148 mm) gives 69 µm/px; the postmark ring gives 67 µm/px. Working scale: 65–75 µm/px.
  • Fibers stay sub-pixel. Paper cellulose fibers run 10–30 µm wide, which is 0.13–0.46 px at this scale. Resolving them would take roughly 2500 ppi. The conclusion is unchanged; the margin is thinner than the old numbers implied.
  • Conservative texture pass. Blank paper patches on both substrates, illumination detrended: correlated 1–2 px mottle on both at similar strength, isotropic, no directional fiber signature, no JPEG block signature. There is no separable “fiber channel”, only generic grain.
  • Reversing the grain. Background subtraction does nothing (raggedness 78.4 → 80.0): the raggedness is baked into the ink–paper boundary, not the background field. Grayscale top-hat shatters strokes. What works is binary morphological opening (r=1) on the Bands mask: postcard edge raggedness 78.4 → 48.5 (38% smoother) at 89.5% ink retention, which puts the postcard past raw onion-skin smoothness (71.1). The grain’s footprint is 1–2 px boundary protrusions where ink feathered into board fibers; opening removes exactly that.
Candidate lab feature. A despeckle toggle (binary opening r=1) on the Bands tab would give this to every image. Not yet implemented.
07

Methods and reproducibility

  • Sweep A (dotted rows): Chroma 105/8-style matrix, 27 combos (centers 85–125 step 5 × widths 4/8/16, Mask) + Saturation/Hue views at best center; Directional Rows 16 combos (k 1.0/1.5/2.0/2.5 × window 15/31/47/61); Gabor ink-axis 4 combos (orientation 0, λ 4/6/8/12, gain 6); Ridge dark 3 combos (sensitivity 0.01/0.02/0.05).
  • Sweep B (3D): 3D stack × 7 algorithms (Bands, A2 gate, Chroma, Direct., Emboss, Raking, Gabor) × Window/Advanced (12 layers, sheet spacing 35); Join × 5 algorithms (defaults: 12 layers, sweep 60–160, ridge threshold 0.5, target ink, min span 2).
  • Sweep C (39 + Berliner): per image: Chroma 21, Gabor 8 (luminance, 0/45/90/135 × λ6/10, gain 6), A2 18 (Blue/Black × lt 80/100/120 × st 100/150/200, Kept), Directional 4 (Rows/Cols × k 1.5/2.5, win 31), Emboss 4 (cutoff 8/16 × angle 0/90), Raking 4 (azimuth 0/90/180/270, gain 5), 3D stack 7 (Advanced, 12 layers).
  • Sweep D (substrates): per image: Bands n=12 Window sweep positions 0–11; Chroma 105/8 then 20/8 Mask; Gabor luminance 0/45/90 λ8 gain 6; Directional Rows k 1.5 win 31.
  • Dot trace: local replication of the lab’s chroma mask (pure hue gate), connected-component dot extraction, RANSAC-style row fitting, edge-distance test vs the block boundary. Script: blot-lab/scripts/dot_trace.py.
  • Grain audit: local scripts only; Bands replicated exactly (LAB L*, 2nd–98th percentile range, 12 bands, Window width 3). Results: blot-lab/results/fiber-noise-and-scale.md.
  • Viewer runs were read-only; sliders driven by keyboard. Per-permutation notes: blot-lab/results/ (3d-stack-scan17.md, dotted-line-scan17.md, berliner-39blot.md, uploads-postcards-onionskin.md).
  • Lab build: blot-lab site, 14 Oct 2026. The five substrate images were added as built-in samples in this build.
