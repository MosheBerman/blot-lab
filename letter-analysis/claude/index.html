Summary

#

54 scans (110 page images) were examined; 45 have Helen Hunter transcriptions and were run through every text test. No mechanical cipher was found: skip sequences, acrostics, capitals, Caesar and anagram tests all sit at chance. No faint or dotted hidden writing was found. What the letters do contain is plain-sight writing: places named through relatives, deportation called “moving”, detention called “not at home”, a destination replaced by a name. Negative results

#
  • Skip sequences: across all 45 transcribed letters, 2,997 hits vs 2,947.5 expected from shuffled letters (aggregate z = 0.88); no single letter beyond |z| = 2. No hidden word stands out.
  • Acrostics, telestichs, first/last words, word and sentence initials, capitals, Bacon 5-bit, Caesar 1–25, number-pair book cipher: nothing above the shuffled baseline. Two Caesar hits (L26 “angst”, L30 “krank”) occur at the rate seen in shuffled text.
  • Code-name lexicon (Hebrew/Yiddish words dressed as relatives — Mawes, Rachmones, Zores, Onkel Hunger, Haman …): zero hits in all transcriptions.
  • Lighter words: no letter has an isolated faint word; every lighter region is a fold, glare, show-through, a pencil/copying-pencil tone change or a second sitting.
  • Dots and pinholes under letters: none in a pattern; flagged dots are i-dots, question-mark dots, foxing, stamp ink.
  • Kiss-count zeros vs the letter’s date: 1 match in 7 dated letters (L7: 19 zeros, 19 Jan) — chance level.
  • Anagrams: only ordinary German pairs (hier/ihre, euer/eure, meine/einem, deine/diene); L44 “Gerson/sorgen” is coincidence.

Cross-letter tests

#

Gezeirah shava (shared rare phrases). Word pairs/triples occurring in only 2–3 letters were listed (509 raw; formula greetings removed). The meaningful links are semantic, not lexical: “in der Nähe von dein Bruder Schlojme” (L12, L26) — place by relative; “übersiedeln” (L19, L36) — deportation listing; “besuchen” for Josef’s movements (L11, L18, L43, L52); “zur Mali fahren” for Palestine (L25, L33, L39, L46, L53); “Erholung” (L35 ambiguous; L42 literal). A lexical link that is false: “nicht aufgeben” (L10 give up; L12 post a letter).

Examiner numbers. Identical numbers bind letters into mailing batches: 1820–2622 on L25 and L27; 1398a on L14 and L39; 1517 on L17 and L23; 1834 on L48 (and probably L52). These are censorship handling numbers, not messages.

Mail route reconstructed from the letters themselves: Josef → Chaja in Vienna (L48) → New York; money and clothes to Josef → Salomon/Schlojme in Strzyżów (L34, L51, L52); parcels → G. Gerhardi c/o Norddeutscher Lloyd, Bremen (L40); tickets and food → HIAS Lisbon (L18, L23, L45).

Shared sheet. L53 and L54 are two sides of one sheet; the L53 deletion shows through on L54 and was used as an independent second look.

Method

#
TechniqueWhat was run
i. Visible textRe-read every page image against Helen Hunter’s transcription and footnotes; corner numbers, stamps, margins, insertions, corrections.
ii. Lighter wordsInk-darkness map per page (pixel darkness vs local paper, LAB) and a cell test: each 3×1.2 x-height text cell compared with its 4×2 neighbourhood (robust z < −3 with normal ink amount). Ink-hue map for second inks.
iii. Subtle marksIsolated ink specks outside word blobs classed by position (below a word, isolated) plus pinhole-like bright specks; zoomed crop sheets reviewed by eye. Dense ink masses (scribble-outs, blots) colour-matched against the darkest nearby writing.
iv. Word skipsEquidistant letter sequences, skips 2–120, both directions, 125 target words (places, family names, danger words, Yiddish code words) vs 40 letter shuffles; every n-th word n = 2–9.
v. AnagramsSame letters, different order: pairs inside each letter; words that rearrange or reverse into a target word.
vi. Jewish code patternsGezeirah shava (rare word pairs/triples shared by only 2–3 letters), notarikon/abbreviations (ב"ה, G.s.d., Ka"h), gematria-notable numbers, Hebrew/Yiddish code names for relatives.
vii. CiphersAcrostic, telestich, first/last words of lines, word and sentence initials, capitals string and Bacon 5-bit, Caesar 1–25, numbers as (line, word) book cipher.
viii. WWI Austrian conventionsPlain-sight techniques known from k.u.k. field-post censorship and reused 1939–42: a place named through a relative (“near brother X”), illness as excuse for silence, “visiting” for movement, euphemisms (“not at home”), substitution of a destination by a name, messages under stamps. Checked by reading.
