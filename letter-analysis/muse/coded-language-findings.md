# Coded-language forensic findings — the Bermann/Lehrhaupt letters

**Date of examination:** 2026-10-16
**Method:** Nine worker subagents examined 53 scanned letters (letters 1–54, no 3.pdf) in batches of 5–7, applying 8 technique families to every letter: (i) visible text, (ii) lighter words, (iii) subtle markings, (iv) word skips/layout, (v) anagram-like pairs, (vi) Jewish religious code patterns, (vii) cryptography, (viii) historical comparison. Sources used: German transcripts (`trans/transcription-N.txt`), Helen Hunter translations with footnotes (`letters/translation-N.docx`, footnotes parsed from `word/footnotes.xml`), scans (`drive/allpdfs/N.pdf`) rendered at 170 dpi and read page by page, and the index CSV. Fresh eyes throughout: no prior analysis files were read, no context graph consulted. All mechanical tests (sentence/line initials, last letters, word-initial triples, strict anagram scans) were run per letter and came back negative unless noted.
**Bottom line:** No hidden or coded message was found in any letter under any technique family. The letters are plain family correspondence; their plain-sight content is, however, dense with emigration logistics, money transfers, parcel routes, and visa paperwork — much of it primary-source-productive. The genuine forensic observations are physical: redactions, re-inkings, blots, filing numbers, and a small number of caution-veiled passages that read as sensitivity, not cipher.

Worker reports (full detail) are preserved in `workers/w1-letters-1-7.md` … `workers/w9-letters-49-54.md`; crops referenced below live in `workers/crops/`, `workers/renders/w9/`, and `workers/w8-scans/`.

## Letter 13 — 1923-03-25 (reissued 1939-10-26), IKG Wien Trauungs-Zeugnis

**Josef Lehrhaupt recte Bermann & Chawa Eva Willner false Katz.** 1 page (+ blank back). Verdict: official certificate, no coded content; the 26.10.1939 reissue date places it in the emigration-paperwork chain.

**i) Visible text.** Method: read transcript/translation/HH footnotes against scan. Observations: marriage 25.3.1923, register "Sch Reihe Zahl 19"; groom b. 15.1.1897 Wiznitz, parents Perez Lehrhaupt/Sara Bermann, Wien II Rueppgasse 18; bride b. 10.5.1895 Brzozow [HH footnote 1], parents Markus Moses Katz/Malke Willner, Wien II Zirkusgasse 11; witnesses Jakob Willner, Bilko Schaje; Rabbi Sigmund Fürst, Große Schiffgasse 8 [HH footnote 2: Schiffschul]. Reissued 26.10.1939 — obtained in the emigration-paper push just before Ewa's Jan/Feb 1940 emigration. Finding: the document IS a primary source; the reissue date is itself an emigration-logistics fact. Confidence: high.
**ii) Lighter words.** Uniform print + handwriting; nothing. Confidence: high.
**iii) Subtle markings.** Red/purple round stamp "MATRIKELAMT DER ISRAELITISCHEN KULTUSGEMEINDE IN WIEN", registrar's signature flourish, faint large circular IKG seal mid-page, one foxing dot above "Bräutigam:". Official marks only. Confidence: high.
**iv) Word skips.** Form layout normal; "[text loss]" at bride's birthplace. Nothing. Confidence: high.
**v) Anagrams.** "r[ecte]"/"false" are Latin legal terms (recte = name correction), not transpositions. Confidence: high.
**vi) Religious code.** N/A. **vii) Cryptography.** N/A. **viii) Historical.** Standard IKG reissue; such Oct-1939 reissues served emigration dossiers, consistent with the affidavit threads in letters 8/10/12. Confidence: high.

## Letter 31 — 1940-01-14, Mali Bermann → Onkel Jakob Willner

Verdict: no coded message. The most historically explicit letter of the corpus (summons, ship tickets, a dated sailing, affidavit-seeking); religious formulas are dense but standard.

**i) Visible text.** Method: transcript + translation + HH footnote vs scans. Observations: "sie hat zwar schon die Vorladung zu Untersuchung, aber jetzt wird man nur vorgenommen wenn man schon die Schifffskarte hat. Und von wo soll sie sie nehmen?" ("although she has got a summons to the examination interview they will only see people now who already have a ship ticket — and how is she supposed to get one of those?"). "daß die Kinder שי׳ am 1. Dec[ember] nach Amerika gefahren sind. Auch hofft sie dieser Tage zu fahren. Was mit Papa sein wird weiß ich nicht." ("the children [she'yichyu] left for America on 1 December and she is hoping to set off too in the next few days. I don't know what will happen with Papa."). "Ich habe gleich nach Erhalt dieses Briefes zu Tante Erna nach Amerika geschrieben, daß sie dort bei den Verwandten irgend etwas bewirkt" (wrote to Aunt Erna in America about relatives and the affidavit). "daß es helfen wird wie einen Toten Bankes" ("about as helpful as cupping a corpse", HH footnote 0 — Yiddish idiom for "useless"). "Fini Kaufmann ist beigekommen, ich weiß aber nicht wo sie sich aufhält" ("Fini Kaufmann came by, but I don't know where she is staying"). Hebrew header top-right of p.1: "ב״ה, יום א׳..." ("with G-d's help, Sunday..."), a standard Orthodox letter opening. Finding: explicit emigration logistics under Nazi bureaucracy, stated plainly — the opposite of coded. "שי׳" is the standard she'yichyu blessing, not a signal. Confidence: medium-high.
**ii) Lighter words.** Blue ink with regular darker-lighter line alternation — fountain-pen re-dip pattern; "G-tt"/"G-ttes" with omitted 'o' (divine-name orthography). Nothing systematic. Confidence: high.
**iii) Subtle markings.** Tiny reddish dot above the "Mali" signature [uncertain — likely scan artifact]; torn left edge with text loss (transcript-marked, physical damage, not redaction). Nothing else. Confidence: medium.
**iv) Word skips.** Torn-edge text loss; the 23-zero number on its own line; otherwise standard. Nothing coded. Confidence: high.
**v) Anagrams.** Zero pairs; "Mali" vs "Mala" are diminutive variants of "Malka" across letters. Confidence: high.
**vi) Religious code.** "ב״ה" header, "שי׳", "G-tt", "Mit G-ttes Hilfe", "Toten Bankes" idiom — every item a standard Orthodox formula, naturally placed; no gezeirah-shava linkage, no gematria. Confidence: high.
**vii) Cryptography.** Sentence-initials, line-initials, last letters all listed — no words/names. The zero-string "100000000000000000000000" counts 23 zeros (10^23): hyperbole, not gematria. Confidence: high.
**viii) Historical.** Everything stated plainly in Jan 1940; no jargon code with recoverable meanings. Notable for its explicitness. Confidence: medium.

## Letter 33 — 1940-02-11, Josef Bermann (+ Klara, Mali, third author) → Ewa Bermann

Verdict: no coded message. Notable: reversed-orientation third-author marginalia, a retraced "cheder", the penciled "1463", and a Kalman-relayed greeting.

**i) Visible text.** Observations: "daß sollst alles mögliche einwenden und mir eine Ausreisemöglichkeit verschaffen nach Amerika" ("you must do everything you can to try and find some way for me to leave the country [for America]"); "ich habe einen Gruß bei der Kalman von der Mali erhalten" ("I got a message from Mali in a letter from Kalman" — greeting relayed via a third party's letter); "Ist Jo[el?] schon in Amerika denn hat vorige Woche geschriebe daß er das Visum erhalten hat und wird demnachst fahren" (HH footnote 0: name end obscured by blot; Joel inferred); "Taube Ettels [Peretz] wird so G[ott] will demachst nach America fahren auch die Frau Dornhelm wird hoffentlich in 14 Tage fahren. sie hat schon das Visum bekomen" (Peretz and Frau Dornhelm's travel, visa obtained); "Ruchel Lea wohnt bei mir" (residence fact); "Berta hat auch schon schon lange ihrer Mutter nicht geschrieben" (doubled "schon" — a slip). Finding: visa/travel facts are primary-source-relevant (Joel's visa, Dornhelm's visa, Peretz's planned sailing). The Kalman-relayed greeting is ordinary wartime-post relay, not deception design. Confidence: medium.
**ii) Lighter words.** Josef's text dark brown-black; p.2 shows large washed-out gray patches following moisture-damage contours, not word boundaries; the p.1 margins' third-author note is a different author's lighter lavender/blue ink, not lighter words within one hand. No deliberate lighter words. Confidence: medium.
**iii) Subtle markings.** "cheder" in "gehen die fleißig in die Schule wie auch cheder" written/retraced in noticeably darker ink — emphasis or retracing; penciled "1463" bottom-left of p.1 (different hand, likely archival or censor processing number); "Euch" in the closing carries a dot/mark; digit string "10000000000000000" (17 characters) after "kusse"; an ink blot obscures the end of "Jo[el?]" on p.2 (HH footnote 0 — most plausibly accidental). Finding: emphasis/processing marks only. Confidence: medium (observations); low (coded meaning).
**iv) Word skips.** The third author's note ("beste grusse der Schwester Rosa un[d] F[a]m[i]l[ie] ...", signed vertically along the left edge "R [?] Korn") is written into the top and bottom margins of p.1 in reverse (upside-down) orientation in the lighter blue ink — the sheet was turned 180° and the empty margins used. The third author's main letter is on the reverse of p.3, which was not scanned (CSV confirms). The note's content is an ordinary greeting — no concealment of substance. Confidence: medium.
**v) Anagrams.** "Berta" (Josef) vs "Bertha" (third author) — inconsistent h-spelling of the same name within the one letter; "Malin" is a declined form of "Mali" (HH footnote 1); "Chawcze/Chafze/Chajtsche" are dialect variants across authors. No true permutation pairs. Confidence: high.
**vi) Religious code.** "cheder", "G[ott] s[ei] d[ank]", "so G[ott] will" — idiomatic, natural. Confidence: medium.
**vii) Cryptography.** Josef's 19 sentence-initials ("E w C H W W k C I d D B I B R P s s E") and Klara/Mali's 7 ("E b a i W w G") form no words; last letters/word-initials nothing; no book-cipher references. Confidence: high.
**viii) Historical.** February 1940 Vienna — emigration, not deportation; "Ausreisemöglichkeit", "Einreise", "Visum", "fahren" all literal. Confidence: medium.

## Letter 46 — 1940-02-12 / 1940-04-25, Josef Bermann (+ 2–3 other authors) → Chava/Ewa

Verdict: no coded message. A multi-author mail packet; corrections, blots, filing stamps, and one illegible scribbled-out header are ordinary writing/archival artifacts. The historically loaded word "Transport" appears — in 1940, an emigration transport, stated plainly.

**i) Visible text.** Observations: 12 Feb (Josef): "ich habe von vielen Leuten gehört Wann man minderjahrige in America hat besteht eine Möglichkeit daß die Kinder können ihre Eltern anfordern so daß die Quote einfach entfällt oder kommt man in einer bevorzugte Quote" (children in America can petition for their parents — quota route); "Die Frau Dornhelm hat auch zur Untersuchung hoffentlich fahr sie auch"; "du sollst zur Frau Fli[ck] gehen" [uncertain]; "da wurde Adi aufgenommen durch die Empfehlung von Herzog" (Adi placed through Herzog's recommendation). 25 Apr (Josef): "sage der Mali sie soll dem Perez aufsuchen und ihm erinnern, daß er der Ruchel Lea schreiben soll denn sie ist sehr nachdenklich über sein so langes Schweigen" (a message to be sent); "Ruchel Lea wohnt bei mir auch Taube Ettel [will] zu mir herziehen sie wird vielleicht ihre Wohnung aufgeben"; "Von der Schwägerin Riwke haben wir eine Karte an Ruchale angefragt... Nächstens werden wir die Karte beilegen von der Riwke und lieben Kindern" (Riwke's card to be enclosed). "Liebe Schwester!" (other author): "die Frau Felde hat gesagt der H. Fink freut sich sehr das du schon dort bist"; "von H. Kessel Perez war [Hebrew/Yiddish] einschreiben" (registered letter from Mr. Kessel, Perez). Lea Frohlich [?]: "Liebe Berta ich bitte zu sagen meinen lieben Sohn daß ich weiß nicht wohin er hat das [Spesen?] hingeschickt ich kan mit den Transport nicht mehr daran komen" ("tell my dear son I don't know where he sent the [expenses?] — I can no longer make the transport"). Finding: no coded message; the emigration-bureaucracy vocabulary (Quote, Untersuchung, Einreise/Ausreise, Transport, Luftpost) is the plain technical language of 1940 Vienna. Primary-source leads: the 10 Feb 1940 telegram; the quota/children-petition route; Herzog's placement of Adi; Lea's missed transport and Berta's son's "Spesen". Confidence: high.
**ii) Lighter words.** Four hands — Josef's brown ink; "Liebe Schwester!" author's darker pencil-like hand; reverse-orientation author's uniformly faint faded ink; Lea's very dark bold ink. No individual words lighter within any hand. Confidence: high.
**iii) Subtle markings.** P.2 header: "Wien 25. April 1940" over a scribbled-out line; a full line between greeting and "Den Brief vom 16 Feber" scribbled out (likely a first attempt at the date line); "16" corrected (HH: 14 also possible). P.1 top-left (upside-down letter): header block heavily scribbled over — crossed-out address/name header, illegible at available resolution [uncertain]. P.3 quota paragraph: dense crossing-out and interlinear rewriting + two ink blots (pen blobs). P.5: purple stamps "1654" and "764" bottom-left (archival/filing); p.2: "326" penciled (filing number, different hand). P.7 (Lea): large ink blobs near "[Spesen?]" and "Transport" — heavy nib, words readable, no deliberate obliteration. No dots/ticks/margin marks/circles/underlining. Confidence: medium-high.
**iv) Word skips.** Second author's letter written upside-down in remaining space of the folded sheet (space reuse); Lea's letter is the END of a letter whose first page was not scanned; quota-paragraph and "dennen" revisions are normal corrections. Nothing coded. Confidence: high.
**v) Anagrams.** One genuine pair: "Fröste" ("die Fröste größer") vs "öfters" ("sie fragt mich öfters") — true permutation (f,r,ö,s,t,e) but across two different authors and subjects: coincidence. "Chawtsche/Chawcze/Chajcze" natural alternation; "Perez/Peretz" two authors' spellings. Confidence: medium-high.
**vi) Religious code.** Conventional blessings ("soll helfen daß...", "guten Jontew", "der beschafer soll eich imer Segnen"); "die Feiertage" (Passover) natural; the trillion kisses hyperbole. Nothing signal-like. Confidence: high.
**vii) Cryptography.** Sentence-initials, paragraph-initials, finals listed — nothing; no date/number anomalies beyond corrected "16 Feber"; no book-cipher references. Confidence: high.
**viii) Historical.** "Transport" (Lea: "ich kan mit den Transport nicht mehr daran komen") is the historically loaded term — in 1940 Vienna an emigration transport, stated plainly that she cannot make it. Openly anxious, no forced cheer. Confidence: high.

## Letter 4 — 1940-04-07, Klara, Mali, Jossel → Ewa Berman, Jacob Willner

Verdict: no coded message. Ordinary multi-author family letter; the "Liebesgaben Paket" discussion is the concrete logistics fact; one small ink blot is unresolvable but does not affect the reading.

**i) Visible text.** Observations: "mit dem Liebesgaben Paket was Jakob schreibt wan Euch wirklich möglich ist, so bitte sehr" ("regarding the care package that Jakob mentioned, if you really are able to, then yes please" — HH footnote: 'care package' may be anachronistic; the sense is a gift parcel of needed things); "ich freue mich sehr, daß der l[iebe] Joel G[ott] s[ei] d[ank] in Amerika ist, und daß Ihr schon bald [Hebrew/Yiddish] fahren w[unclear]kön[n]en, Gebe [Hebrew/Yiddish] das Ihr eine glückliche Reise haben solt"; the child's quoted question: "sie sagt nur, warum Mutti sind wir nicht mitn Onkel Joel, Onkel Kalman, oder Onkel Jakob gefahren, wir häten können alle nach Amerika dan fahren"; "von die l[iebe] Eva, Kinder, auch l[iebe] Mina habe kein Post bekom[m]en, aber die F[rau] Stern hat von l[ieber] Mina ein Brief und eine Karte erhalten" (third-party mail report); penciled "382/67" top-right of p.1. Finding: no coded message; parcel discussion and emigration-journey context are the facts. Confidence: high.
**ii) Lighter words.** Klara's hand uniformly dark Kurrent; Jossel's two notes in a lighter thinner hand — authorship difference, not selective lightness. Confidence: high.
**iii) Subtle markings.** Heavy authorial corrections in Klara's dark ink: "und, daß" over-dark; "haben" blacked out in "eine glückliche Reise haben solt"; a whole word struck through before "mit dem Liebesgaben Paket"; "Alle" in "läßt Alle schön grüßen" struck/overwritten; on p.3 a small dense ink BLOT between "Können" and "alle" in "wir häten Können [blot] alle nach Amerika dan fahren" — at 300 dpi resolves to a small ink blob, possibly covering a 2–3-letter word, unresolvable; the transcript's reading stands ("alle" is written after the blot). **iv) Word skips.** Jossel's second note ("Lieber Jakob. Ich wünsche Euch eine glückliche Reise und gruße Euch bestens Euer Josel Berman / ich habe mehr kein Zeit zu schreiben") written VERTICALLY along the right margin of the facing page — squeezed in because the page was full (space-maximising, cf. letter 6's rotated sections). Folded sheet with writing in multiple directions; bottom half of p.3 blank with show-through. No skipped words. Confidence: high.
**v) Anagrams.** "Kousin"/"Kusine" — masculine/feminine grammatical pair, not a transposition. Nothing else. Confidence: high.
**vi) Religious code.** Hebrew/Yiddish abbreviations open each section ("[Hebrew/Yiddish]" — ב"ה-style), standard Orthodox epistolary convention, used naturally and repeatedly. No holidays, no liturgical quotes, no gezeirah-shava linkage. Confidence: high.
**vii) Cryptography.** Line-initials, sentence-initials, line-lasts, sentence-lasts listed — no words formed. "382/67" penciled, ordinary. No book-cipher references; mail references ("Dein l[ieben] Brief", "was Jakob schreibt") are ordinary bookkeeping. Misspellings (Kousin, bekomen, zusamen) are normal phonetics. Confidence: high.
**viii) Historical.** Open emigration and parcel discussion under 1940 civilian censorship; no euphemism, no forced-cheer formulas. Confidence: high.

## Letter 24 — 1940-04-10, Josef Bermann (+ Ruchalen) → Ewa Bermann

Verdict: no hidden message. A two-author family letter whose forensic interest is purely material: two inks, multi-directional space-filling layout, one deletion, one accidental ink blot, one fold-note. Content value is as primary-source pointers (20 April 1940 examination appointments; Bertha's missing affidavit; the unidentified "Rojse").

**i) Visible text.** Observations (Josef): "Es wundert mich sehr daß habe von dir noch von den lieben Kinder kein einziges Schreiben bekommen aber von der liebe Regina habe eine Karte bekommen" (mail-gap anxiety; Regina's postcard a referenced other message); "Bist du gesund? Wohnst du in der Nähe von der Mina? Was macht der Onkel überhaupt die ganze Familie? Bekomms du von der liebe Mali Schreiben?" (rapid questions; "der Onkel" and "Mina" = the New York circle); "Die Jetti Lachs hat am 20ten April zur Untersuchung und Ruche Lea. Ich hoffe daß es mir gelingen zur Mali zu kommen" (dated visa-examination appointments — primary-source pointer; "zur Mali zu kommen" = join daughter Mali in America); "Von der Berta hat Ruchel Lea ein Brief bekommen wo sie schreibt daß unsere lieben Kinder bei der lieben Rojse waren zu Besuch. Bitte dich auch du sollst zu ihr hingehen" (Berta's letter referenced; "Rojse" [uncertain — HH: likeliest reading] is an unidentified person, a genealogical lead); "100000000000000 Pussi du kannst den Kindern austeilen statt meiner und wunsche Euch einen lustigen Pesach" (10^14 hyperbolic kisses; "Pussi" = regional "Bussi" per HH; "lustigen Pesach" timely — Passover began the evening of 16 April 1940); "Besondere Grusse an meiner lieben Schwester Rosa und Familie auch lasse die lieben Berta Willi und das suse Kind grussen" ("Willi" here vs. Ruchalen's "Wielen" — HH: likely a declined "Willi" variant). (Ruchalen): "warst du bei mein Bertha warum hat die Bertha kein grus zu geschrieben [deletion]" (deletion unresolvable at 170 dpi); "die Bertha hat geschrieben sie schickt mir Afidawid bis heute keines erhalten" (a promised US affidavit never arrived — primary-source pointer); "Ich habe erhalten fon meine Kinder ein Brief mit grossen sensucht nach 3 Monat auch Fategrafie von Wielen mit den susen Kind bis Hundert Jahr" ("Fategrafie" = Fotografie, "Afidawid" = affidavit — phonetic spellings; "bis Hundert Jahr" the traditional birthday blessing). Finding: family news + emigration logistics (examinations, affidavit, mail gaps). No money amounts, no routes, no deportation euphemisms. The multi-author, multi-direction layout is space-maximizing, not steganography. Confidence: high (Josef); medium (Ruchalen — faint, some [unclear] per HH).
**ii) Lighter words.** Ruchalen's ENTIRE contribution (bottom half, vertical columns, top-edge continuation) is in markedly lighter blue-grey ink than Josef's brown — a different pen/ink, authorship-related, consistent across her whole text. Within each author's text the ink is uniform. No selective lightening. Confidence: high.
**iii) Subtle markings.** One deletion in Ruchalen's text (unresolvable); an irregular dark ink blot with satellite spot at the left edge where Ruchalen's letter begins (accidental spill morphology, obscures nothing critical); no underlines/dots/circles/carets/margin ticks in Josef's text; the fold-note across the central fold is in a smaller darker hand (editorial/explanatory note, per HH). Nothing indicating a marking code. Confidence: medium-high.
**iv) Word skips.** Josef: two normal columns (right then left). Ruchalen: vertical columns at 90° plus an upside-down continuation along the top edge; the fold-note squeezed across the central fold; text blurred along fold lines (per HH). No skipped words, no squeezed insertions, no cross-out-and-rewrite beyond the single deletion. Confidence: high.

## Cross-letter synthesis

**The null result is the headline.** Across 45 examinable letters (all except 3/no-scan, 49/dupe, 28/out-of-scope, 21-22/15-16 partial Yiddish), every mechanical test — sentence/line initials and final letters listed per letter, word-initial triples (Pryor-style), 2nd-letter sequences, strict anagram scans — returned no words, names, or patterns. **What the letters actually are.** Open family correspondence conducting emigration business in plain sight. The recurring vocabulary — "Quote"/"Cwote" (quota), "Untersuchung" (consulate/medical examination), "Affidavit"/"Avidavid"/"Afidawid", "Schiffskarten", "Paket"/"Pakerl", "Fahrkarte", "Visum", "Konsul", "Hias", "Komitee", "Luftpost", "ferdient/verdient" (earning) — is the shared working language of 1940–41 Vienna emigration efforts, not a Decksprache: it appears in letters that demonstrably passed Wehrmacht censorship (letters 17, 19, 23), which is evidence against a hidden layer.
